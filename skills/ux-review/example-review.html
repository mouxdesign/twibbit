1
Say why, how long and how much

Someone who just tapped Receive wants to get paid, not to open a channel. Tell them what a channel is in one plain line, then give the wait and the cost, so there are no surprises. Please fill in the real time and fee. I do not know them.

“First, set up a channel. A channel is your connection to the Lightning network. It takes about [X] minutes and costs [X] sats.”

2
Give the button a label

The button at the bottom shows only a spinning circle. People will wonder if the app is working, stuck or waiting for them. Say what is happening and for how long. I can only see this screen, so please check what the spinner is really doing.

3
Show the notice before the fields

Right now a person fills in an amount and a message, and only then reads that they cannot receive yet. Putting the notice first, in a card, lets them know before they spend effort. It also makes the channel step feel like a normal first step, not an error.

Make the amount labels easier to read

“Amount sat” breaks over two lines, and “sat” is a word many people do not know. Use one line with the unit in brackets. The Guide also suggests letting people pick their unit, so this could become a switch later.

Part 1: Framework review

The Twibbit 10 principles. All ten, with the ones that did not apply to this screen marked.

1. Mistakes are permanentPrevent errors before they happen. Not applied here, there is no payment being sent.
2. Trust is everythingEvery screen answers: is my money safe and is everything okay?
3. Onboarding is a crisis pointThe first run is when people make serious mistakes. See change 3.
4. Jargon kills adoptionEvery jargon word is a person who gives up. See changes 1, 4 and 5.
5. Progressive disclosureShow the simple version first. Not applied here.
6. Extreme user rangeWork for beginners and experts. See change 4.
7. Invisible tech, visible stateAlways show what is happening now. See changes 2 and 3.
8. Security vs usabilityConfirmations should earn their place. Not applied here.
9. Education is part of the UXThe app has to teach as people go. See change 1.
10. Password protectionNobody can reset a lost seed phrase. Not applied here.

First impressions

The words on this screen are short and direct, which is good. But three of them stop a newcomer: Lightning channel, sat and Payer. The screen does not use the same tone throughout. The notice is a full sentence, while the labels are single words.

Two checks I could not finish from a screenshot. The orange labels and the grey hints on the near black background may be hard to read, please check the contrast. And I could not tell what the bottom spinner is waiting for.

On privacy, the “Payer” and “Message” fields suggest the other person may see what you type. Please confirm what is shared, then say it on the screen.

Part 2: Bitcoin Design Guide

Receiving

The Guide says that to receive over Lightning, a person needs a channel with enough room to receive, and that any extra fee for opening one should be shown when they create a request, before they share it. Your screen mentions the channel but not the fee. See change 1. Receiving ↗

Explaining channels

The Blixt case study says newcomers are missing the “why”, and that saying how long opening a channel takes sets a clear expectation. It also suggests short text, because many users are not native English speakers. See change 1. Blixt case study ↗

Units

For Lightning wallets the Guide suggests satoshi as the default unit, a way for people to pick their own unit, and an easy way to show local currency. Your screen already shows sats and dollars. See change 4. Units and symbols ↗
